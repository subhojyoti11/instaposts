Support a Lifter.

🦴
PASSIVE FRAME
No power at all
Goal
Redistribute a load toward the ground.
Focus

Result

🔌
TETHERED POWER
Force from an outlet
Goal
Drive strong actuators from an external supply.
Focus
A cable or wheeled cart feeds constant power.
Result
Real assistance, but mobility is gone.
🔋
UNTETHERED BATTERY
A shift on your back

Carry a full day of power on the body.
Focus
Dense cells, efficient actuators, timed bursts.
Result
Powered help that walks anywhere the worker does.

Caption
A powered exoskeleton's hardest problem was never building strong enough motors or a frame that moves with the body. It was carrying enough energy to run those motors for an entire working shift without a cable to the wall. A passive exosuit, built from springs and linkages, redistributes a load toward the ground and reduces strain, but it can never add force of its own, so the amount of help it offers is fixed and modest. Adding powered actuators changes that, but early powered suits had to draw their power from an external supply, a wall outlet or a wheeled cart, which made them useful for a fixed workstation and useless for anyone who needs to walk around. An untethered powered suit is only practical if a single charge lasts a full day. What made that viable is a steady improvement in battery energy density, more usable watt-hours packed into a weight a person can comfortably carry, combined with more efficient actuators and smarter control. A modern assistive exosuit does not run its motors continuously. Sensors in the suit detect the start of a lift or a stride, the actuators deliver a short burst of torque at the hip, knee, or lower back exactly when that joint is under load, and between movements the draw falls to almost nothing. Spending energy only in well timed bursts is what stretches one battery across an eight hour shift. The payoff shows up in two places. On factory floors and in warehouses, powered exosuits reduce the repetitive back and shoulder strain that drives a large share of workplace injuries. In physical therapy, the same class of hardware guides a patient's legs through a correct walking pattern over and over, which is central to relearning gait after a stroke or a spinal injury. The motors and the frame were solved years ago. The suit only became something you could wear all day once the power to run it fit on your back. #robotics #exoskeleton #wearabletech #engineering #ravenextechnologies
