Quick

Neuromorphic chip
Hardware designed around spiking, event-driven neurons instead of dense, clock-driven matrix multiplication.
Spiking Neural Network
A network where neurons accumulate charge and fire discrete spikes only once a threshold is crossed.
Event-driven computation
Power is spent only when a spike happens, not on every clock cycle regardless of activity.
Sparsity
Most neurons stay silent at any given moment, which is exactly where the energy savings come from.

Caption
A single GPU training a large model draws hundreds of watts, continuously, whether or not the input changes much from one instant to the next. Your brain runs perception, memory, and reasoning on roughly 20 watts. The gap is not because biology found better transistors. It is because your brain never computes on a clock. Every CPU and GPU is built on the von Neumann model, shuttling data between memory and compute at a fixed rate. A neural network layer runs its full matrix multiply every cycle, dense and constant, regardless of how much of that signal is actually meaningful. An always-on camera or microphone processing mostly static scenes still pays full compute cost for every frame, which is exactly why edge devices burn through battery so fast. Neuromorphic chips flip this by modeling neurons the way biology actually does it, as leaky integrators that fire discrete spikes. A Spiking Neural Network neuron accumulates input current over time, and that charge continuously leaks away if nothing new arrives. Only when accumulated charge crosses a threshold does the neuron emit a spike and reset. Between spikes, there is no computation and no power draw at all. Downstream neurons only do work when a spike actually reaches them. Sparsity is not an optimization bolted on afterward, it is the entire mechanism. This is a real tradeoff, not a free upgrade. Spikes are discrete and non-differentiable, so training these networks with standard backpropagation needs workarounds like surrogate gradients, and the ecosystem is far less mature than standard deep learning. What you gain in exchange is dramatic: Intel's Loihi 2 idles at around a milliwatt, and IBM's TrueNorth packs a million spiking neurons onto a single chip while drawing a fraction of an equivalent GPU workload's power. The fit is obvious for anything that is mostly quiet punctuated by rare, important events: always-on sensors, event cameras, low-power robotics, hearing aids. Anywhere a device needs to watch constantly but act rarely, spending energy only at the moment something actually happens is the entire point. #neuromorphiccomputing #spikingneuralnetworks #edgeai #computerhardware #ai #ravenextechnologies
