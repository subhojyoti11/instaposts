How Your Ears
Encode a Direction.

A sound is reshaped by your head, shoulders, and the folds of your outer ear
That reshaping differs by direction: earlier and louder on the near side, filtered by the ear's ridges
An HRTF captures this as a per-direction filter for each ear
Software applies the matching filter so a game sound arrives as if from a real spot

Your brain learned your ears' filter over a lifetime. Spatial audio doesn't add a direction channel, it recreates the exact colouring your ears would give a real sound.

Caption
Your ability to tell where a sound is coming from, above you, behind you, close or far, does not come from having two ears alone. It comes from the specific shape of your head, your shoulders, and especially the ridges and folds of your outer ear. As a sound arrives from a given direction, those surfaces delay it, bounce it, and filter certain frequencies before it reaches your eardrum, and the exact pattern of that colouring is different for every direction. Your brain has spent your whole life learning to read it. Plain stereo cannot reproduce any of this. It only adjusts the balance between a left and a right channel, so a sound can slide along a line between two speakers and nowhere else. Generic 3D audio does better by applying an average model of a human head and ears, which is why some sounds feel like they are outside your head and others do not quite. The real fix is a head-related transfer function, an HRTF: a filter, measured or scanned for each ear and for hundreds of directions, that captures exactly how your anatomy would reshape a sound coming from each angle. Software applies the filter matching a sound's virtual position, so a footstep in a game reaches your ears with the same fingerprint a real footstep from that spot would carry. Add head tracking, so the sound stays fixed in the room as you turn, and the brain fully accepts it. The friction is getting your personal HRTF. A proper measurement needs an anechoic chamber, so most consumer systems estimate one from a photo of your ear or a phone scan, which is close but not exact. Directional hearing was never a feature of the speaker. It is a feature of your ears, and spatial audio just recreates what they already do. #spatialaudio #hrtf #audio #spatialcomputing #ravenextechnologies
