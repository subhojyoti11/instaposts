Caption
A robot can watch an egg with a perfect camera and still crack it, because seeing an object and feeling how hard you are squeezing it are two completely different problems. Vision handles the first half of manipulation well: locate the object, plan an approach, position the gripper. Then contact happens, fingers close around the object, and the camera's view gets occluded at the exact moment the hardest part of the task begins. Force sensing at the wrist or motor fills part of that gap, reporting total grip torque, but it has no idea how that force is distributed across the fingertip, or whether the object is a hard-shelled walnut or a soft grape. Optical tactile sensors, the approach popularized by MIT's GelSight, solve this by putting a tiny camera inside the fingertip itself. A soft, clear elastomer pad sits at the point of contact and deforms like real skin when it touches something. Internal LEDs illuminate that gel from inside the sensor housing, and a miniature camera photographs exactly how the surface is bending around the object. Software converts that deformation image into a detailed 3D contact map, capturing shape, texture, and pressure distribution at a resolution vision alone could never provide from three feet away. The most useful trick this unlocks is slip detection. When an object begins sliding out of a grip, the contact pattern inside the gel image itself visibly shifts, showing up as motion the same way optical flow tracks movement in ordinary video. Detect that shift early enough, and the gripper can tighten its hold before the object actually falls, catching a failure mode that is functionally invisible to any camera watching from outside the hand. This is precisely what makes tasks like threading a needle, peeling a single sheet of paper off a stack, or gently gripping fragile produce possible for a robot. None of it works from vision alone. It requires a sense that only exists at the point of contact. #tactilesensors #robotics #gelsight #manipulation #embodiedai #ravenextechnologies
