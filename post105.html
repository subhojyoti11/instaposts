Caption
A laser can identify a single weed among thousands of healthy crop plants and destroy just that one, without a drop of herbicide touching the field. That is not a lab demo anymore, it is running on rovers in commercial fields today. Conventional weed control still mostly means spraying an entire field uniformly, regardless of where the actual weeds are. It works, but at a real cost. Blanket application kills weeds and stresses soil biology across acres a single weed problem never touched, and decades of repeated spraying have bred weed populations that are now genetically resistant to the most common herbicides. A sprayer has no way to tell a healthy crop row from an invasive weed growing inches away, it simply treats the whole field the same. Drone scouting was the first real improvement, flying multispectral cameras over fields to build a live map of exactly where weeds and crop stress actually exist. That solves the information problem, you finally know where the weeds are, but scouting alone doesn't remove anything. The real shift happens on the ground, where laser-weeding rovers drive down crop rows with cameras capturing every individual plant at close range. A vision model classifies each plant in real time, crop or weed, often down to the specific species, and for every plant labeled a weed, the rover aims a low-power laser precisely at its meristem, the plant's growing point, and fires a brief pulse that stops the weed without touching a single neighboring crop plant. The result is mechanical weed control with zero herbicide and millimeter-level precision, at a scale of well over a hundred thousand individual plant decisions per acre pass. Because no chemicals ever touch the field, laser-weeded crops can qualify for organic certification that herbicide use would immediately disqualify. And a laser cannot be evolved around the way herbicide resistance evolves, it kills mechanically, plant by plant, not through a chemical a population can adapt to. Farming spent a century treating the field as one uniform surface. Computer vision finally lets it treat every single plant as its own decision. #agtech #precisionagriculture #computervision #sustainablefarming #ravenextechnologies
