Caption
A page can rank first on Google and still get zero visits, because the search results page increasingly answers the question itself before anyone has a reason to click anything. Traditional SEO optimized for a specific outcome, ranking high enough on a page of blue links that people would click through to your site. That still matters, but it's no longer the finish line, because when an AI-generated summary sits above those links and directly answers the query, plenty of searchers never scroll past it at all. Publishing more pages to cover more queries doesn't solve this either, since a broad library of generic content gives an answer engine nothing worth citing specifically, it can just summarize the general consensus without pointing at any single source. What actually earns visibility now is content an AI answer engine has a real reason to draw from, which is the core idea behind GEO, generative engine optimization. That means original case studies and firsthand data a model can't invent or find duplicated elsewhere, content structured directly around the literal questions people are typing into a search or chat box rather than around a keyword, and dedicated FAQ-style sections that state a direct answer plainly instead of burying it in paragraphs. All three of those formats share one trait, they're specific and extractable, exactly the qualities that make a piece of content easy for a model to quote or cite precisely instead of paraphrasing generically. None of this replaces technical SEO fundamentals like crawlability, site structure, or page speed, those still matter for getting indexed and considered at all. Attribution from AI answers is also still inconsistent today, not every generated response cites its sources clearly, which makes citability a real but evolving signal rather than a guaranteed payoff. Ranking first was never really the goal. Being worth quoting is what actually earns attention now. #seo #geo #contentstrategy #ai #ravenextechnologies
