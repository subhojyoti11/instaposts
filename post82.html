The GAN
Problem.

Generator
Fakes image
Tries to fool the discriminator
⇄
Discriminator
Judges it
Tries to catch the fake

Two networks fighting for dominance. If the discriminator gets too strong, the generator's gradients vanish and it stops learning entirely. This is called mode collapse, and it happens constantly.

Instability
Adversarial training has no guaranteed convergence. Two networks chasing a moving target regularly oscillate instead of settling.
Mode collapse
The generator finds one output that fools the critic and repeats it. Diversity of samples quietly disappears.

03 / 07

Learn to Undo
Noise.

Diffusion flips the problem. Instead of one network faking images past a critic, a single network learns one narrow skill: given a noisy image, predict the noise that was added.
Forward process
Add a little Gaussian noise to a real image, 1000 times, until it is pure static.
Train the denoiser
A U-Net learns to predict the noise at every step, given the noisy image and the timestep.
Reverse process
Start from pure static and subtract the predicted noise, step by step, until an image emerges.

No adversary. No second network to destabilize training. Just a single, well-posed regression problem: predict the noise. Mean squared error, gradient descent, done.

05 / 07

GAN vs
Diffusion.

Training
GAN: unstable, two networks racing each other. Diffusion: a stable, single-network regression loss.
Sampling speed
GAN: one forward pass, near-instant. Diffusion: 20 to 1000 denoising steps, historically much slower.
Diversity
GAN: prone to mode collapse, repeats safe outputs. Diffusion: covers the full data distribution far more reliably.
Image quality
Diffusion models now beat GANs on FID score across nearly every benchmark. That gap is what ended the era of GAN-based generation.

07 / 07

Quick
Reference.

Diffusion model
Learns to reverse a gradual noising process to turn static into an image, one denoising step at a time.
GAN
A generator and discriminator trained adversarially. Fast to sample, notoriously unstable to train.
Latent diffusion
Runs the diffusion process in a compressed latent space instead of raw pixels, cutting compute by roughly 4x.
Classifier-free guidance
A weighting trick that trades sample diversity for prompt fidelity. Higher CFG means closer to the text, less variety.

Caption
Three years ago, every AI image generator you used was a GAN. Today, none of them are. A Generative Adversarial Network pits two neural networks against each other. A generator makes fake images, a discriminator tries to catch the fakes, and the two improve by competing. It is an elegant idea, and it is brutally hard to train. If the discriminator gets even slightly ahead, the generator's gradients vanish and learning stalls. Push too hard the other way and you get mode collapse, where the generator finds one output that reliably fools the critic and just repeats it. Entire papers exist purely on stabilization tricks, because the base setup fights itself by design. Diffusion models solve a different, much simpler problem. Take a real image and add a little Gaussian noise, over and over, a thousand times, until nothing recognizable remains. Now train a single network to reverse one step of that process, predicting the noise that was added at a given timestep. That is the entire training objective, a plain regression loss with no adversary and no second network to destabilize anything. Run it backward from pure static, subtracting predicted noise step by step, and an image gradually resolves out of the noise. The tradeoff used to be speed. A GAN samples in one forward pass. Early diffusion models needed a thousand denoising steps, which made them impractical for real products. Two fixes changed that. Latent diffusion, the technique behind Stable Diffusion, runs the entire denoising process in a compressed latent space instead of raw pixels, cutting compute roughly fourfold. Faster samplers cut the step count from a thousand down to twenty or fifty with barely any quality loss. What sealed it was quality. Diffusion models cover the full data distribution far more reliably than GANs, and they now beat GANs on FID score across nearly every image benchmark that matters. Stable Diffusion, Midjourney, DALL-E 3, and Google's Imagen are all diffusion models wearing different frontends. The architecture war is over. Diffusion won because a stable, well-posed training problem eventually beats a clever, unstable one. #diffusionmodels #generativeai #machinelearning #stablediffusion #deeplearning #ravenextechnologies
