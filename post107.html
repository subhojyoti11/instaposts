The Loop, in
Pseudocode.

# One scheduling tick across the grid's active bots def grid_step(bots, order_queue): paths = [plan_path(b, order_queue) for b in bots] safe_paths = resolve_conflicts(paths) for bot, path in zip(bots, safe_paths): bot.move(path.next_step())

With over a thousand bots sharing the grid, the hard problem was never lifting a bin, it was making sure two bots never claim the same square.

Caption
Over a thousand robots share the same physical grid above a grocery warehouse, passing within centimeters of each other continuously, and none of them ever collide. That is not a safety margin, it is the entire point of the design. Traditional warehouses store inventory on shelves accessed by aisles, and human pickers walk to each item. It's flexible, but most of a picker's shift is spent walking between shelves, not actually picking anything. Conveyor-based sortation improves throughput by moving items along mechanical belts to central sorting points, but the physical layout is permanently fixed once built, expensive and disruptive to ever reconfigure. Both approaches also waste most of a warehouse's vertical space, since a person or forklift can only comfortably reach so high. Ocado's grid system removes aisles and shelving entirely. Grocery totes are stacked directly on top of each other in dense columns, and robots travel on a rail system across the top of the grid, dropping a claw down into the stack to lift out whichever bin an order needs. The genuinely hard engineering problem was never lifting the bin, it was coordinating over a thousand robots sharing that same grid without any of them colliding. A central scheduler continuously plans a path for every active bot and resolves near-collisions in real time, letting bots pass within centimeters of each other on a live, constantly shifting traffic pattern. Once a bot retrieves the right bin, it surfaces at a picking station where a human, not a robot, places the specific item into a customer's actual order, the one part of the process still faster and more reliable done by a person. The result is a warehouse that fulfills thousands of grocery orders per hour from a physical footprint a conventional aisled warehouse would need to be far larger to match. The breakthrough here isn't really robotics, lifting a bin is easy. It's scheduling, keeping a thousand independent robots moving through the exact same space without a single one ever colliding. #warehouseautomation #robotics #logistics #ocado #ravenextechnologies
