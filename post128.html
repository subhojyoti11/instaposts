Caption
Text written by an AI model can now carry a fingerprint you cannot see, cannot edit out by reformatting, and cannot strip the way you'd strip a file's metadata, because the mark was never attached to the file at all. The obvious first approach was metadata, a small attached tag describing where a file came from, the same idea behind a photo quietly recording its location and device. It fails almost immediately as a real safeguard, because metadata is trivially removable, and the moment AI-generated text gets copied and pasted into another document, whatever tag existed is already gone. Analyzing writing style to guess whether text came from a model is unreliable too, easy enough to disguise with light editing that changes surface patterns without changing meaning. What actually survives copying and editing is a watermark baked into the words themselves. A language model doesn't know facts, it predicts one next word at a time from a ranked list of likely candidates. A watermarking scheme splits that candidate list into two pseudorandom groups at every single step, informally a green set and a red set, and nudges the model's sampling to slightly favor green-listed words over red ones. No single word choice looks unusual to a reader, the nudge is small and the pattern only becomes visible statistically. But across a full paragraph, watermarked text lands on green-listed words noticeably more than fifty percent of the time, a pattern invisible to a human eye but easily measurable by the model checking its own output. Regulation is the actual reason this exists at scale. The EU AI Act requires AI-generated content to be identifiable, which pushed labs to adopt watermarking broadly rather than leaving it purely voluntary, and some rolled it out globally instead of region by region. Google's SynthID uses a related, more intricate version of the same underlying green and red token idea. Nothing about the text itself ever looked different to you. The pattern was only ever visible in the choices, counted across hundreds of words at once. #llm #aiwatermarking #machinelearning #airegulation #ravenextechnologies
