03 / 07

Three Ways to
Move a Joint.

💧
HYDRAULIC
Pressurized fluid
Goal
Deliver massive force through pressurized fluid.
Focus
A central pump drives fluid through every joint.
Result
Powerful but leaky, loud, imprecise at low speed.
🧵
CABLE-DRIVEN
Tendon routed
Goal
Route motor force through tendons to distant joints.
Focus
Motors stay centralized, cables transmit outward.
Result
Lighter limbs, but cables stretch and wear.
⚡
ELECTRIC
Servo per joint
Goal
Put a precise servo motor directly at every joint.
Focus
Each joint has its own motor, gearbox, controller.
Result
Quiet, precise, and far easier to maintain.
↻ The new Atlas has zero hydraulic lines

Quick
Reference.

Hydraulic actuation
Using pressurized fluid to drive a joint, high force but requiring a continuously running pump.
Electric servo actuator
A self-contained motor, gearbox, and controller placed directly at a single joint.
Dynamic range
How far a joint can rotate or move, a property electric actuators expanded past hydraulic-era limits.
Torque density
Rotational force produced per unit of weight, the metric electric motors finally caught up on.

Caption
For twenty years, the sound of a Boston Dynamics robot was the whir and hiss of a hydraulic pump running constantly, even while the robot stood still. The new Atlas is silent. That silence is the whole story. Hydraulic actuation was never really a choice, it was the only technology that could deliver the raw force robots like Atlas needed to jump, carry heavy loads, and recover from falls. But hydraulics come with real costs. Pressurized fluid lines leak and require constant seal maintenance, sometimes failing catastrophically mid-motion. Maintaining that pressure means a pump has to run continuously, consuming power and producing noise even when nothing is moving. And hydraulic actuators, while excellent at raw force, are notoriously difficult to control precisely at the low speeds where delicate manipulation actually happens. Electric servo actuation solves all three problems by rethinking the joint itself. Instead of one central pump driving fluid through actuators across the whole robot, every joint gets its own self-contained motor, gearbox, and controller. There is no shared pressure line, so one joint failing doesn't threaten the entire robot. Boston Dynamics didn't retrofit this into the old hydraulic frame either, the new Atlas was rebuilt from scratch around electric actuation, and that ground-up redesign is what let engineers give joints a dynamic range that goes well past typical human range of motion, rotations no hydraulic cylinder was ever built to allow. The practical differences show up immediately. Swapping a single electric servo motor takes minutes. Replacing a leaking hydraulic seal used to mean draining an entire pressurized line just to service one joint. And the robot finally moves quietly enough to work somewhere other than a demo stage. For years, more power meant more hydraulics. It turns out the actual constraint was never raw force. It was getting motors precise and dense enough to match it, quietly, one joint at a time. #bostondynamics #robotics #atlas #actuators #ravenextechnologies
