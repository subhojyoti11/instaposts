Three Ways to
Build a Gripper.

🦾
RIGID
Motors + metal
Goal
Precise, repeatable motion using metal links.
Focus
Excellent for fixed, known geometry on a line.
Result
Fast and strong, unsafe near people or fragile items.
🧵
CABLE
Tendon driven
Goal
Mimic tendons pulling joints for some flexibility.
Focus
Cables route force through flexible joints.
Result
More adaptable, but still a rigid skeleton underneath.
🎈
SOFT
Pneumatic
Goal
Replace rigid parts with inflatable silicone chambers.
Focus
The whole limb deforms and conforms, no skeleton.
Result
Naturally safe, naturally adaptive to any shape.

# One control tick for a soft pneumatic gripper def soft_grip(target_pressure, sensor_feedback): pressure = ramp_toward(target_pressure, sensor_feedback) valve.set(pressure) # material compliance does the rest of the work return sensor_feedback.contact_detected

Quick
Reference.

Soft robotics
Robots built primarily from compliant materials like silicone rather than rigid metal links and motors.
Pneumatic actuator
A soft limb that bends or extends when internal chambers are inflated with pressurized air.
Compliance
A material's ability to deform under contact, absorbing impact instead of transmitting full force.
Conforming grip
Wrapping around an object's actual shape to distribute contact force evenly.

Caption
A robot gripper strong enough to lift a car is also strong enough to crush an egg without even trying. For decades that tradeoff seemed unavoidable. Soft robotics exists because it turned out not to be. Traditional grippers are built from rigid metal links driven by motors. They are precise and powerful, exactly what a factory line assembling identical parts wants. But precision and power come at a cost. A rigid gripper applies whatever force its motor produces unless something carefully limits it, and its geometry is fixed, unable to conform to an object that isn't exactly the shape it was designed for. That is also why industrial robot arms typically work inside safety cages, their rigid joints cannot safely absorb an unexpected collision with a person. Soft robotics replaces the rigid skeleton with molded silicone chambers threaded with internal air channels. Pump compressed air into those chambers and the actuator's asymmetric geometry forces it to curl or extend in a specific, predictable direction, the same way a balloon animal takes shape. Because the material itself is soft, an unexpected impact simply deforms the limb instead of transmitting full force into whatever it hit. There is no complex inverse kinematics happening here, the controller just manages air pressure, and the material's own compliance handles the fine positioning. That compliance is exactly what makes soft grippers good at things rigid ones fail at. They are already used on packing lines to handle strawberries and eggs without bruising or cracking them, wrapping around each object's actual shape and distributing contact force evenly instead of pinching at a few hard points. And because the limb genuinely deforms on contact rather than resisting it, soft robots can work directly next to people without the cages rigid arms require. Rigid robotics asked engineers to control force precisely enough to never make a mistake. Soft robotics asks a simpler question, what if the material itself couldn't make a dangerous one. #softrobotics #robotics #pneumatics #humanrobotinteraction #ravenextechnologies
