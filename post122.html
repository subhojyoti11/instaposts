User baseline
A learned profile of typical, individual behavior used as the reference point.
False positive
A legitimate transaction incorrectly flagged as fraudulent.
Real-time scoring
Evaluating and deciding on a transaction's risk within the transaction's own processing window.

Caption
Your bank can flag a stolen card being used on the other side of the world before you've even noticed your wallet is gone, and no human ever looked at the transaction to make that call. Fixed rules, like automatically flagging any purchase over a certain amount made abroad, are simple to write and simple for fraud to eventually learn around, and they treat every customer identically regardless of how that person actually spends. Having a human analyst manually review flagged activity brings real judgment to the decision, but it's far too slow to stop a transaction from settling in real time, the fraud is often long done by the time a person looks at it. What actually protects most card transactions today is anomaly scoring, built from a profile of your own specific spending history, not a generic rulebook. The system learns your typical locations, spending amounts, preferred merchant categories, and even the times of day you usually transact, built up from months of your real, legitimate activity. Every new transaction gets compared against that personal baseline the instant it's submitted, weighing multiple signals together, an unusual location, an unfamiliar merchant category, an odd hour, none of them damning alone but meaningful in combination. Once that combined anomaly score crosses a risk threshold, the transaction gets held or declined before it ever fully settles, all within a fraction of a second. The real tradeoff banks constantly tune is how aggressive that threshold should be, since setting it too sensitive starts blocking your own legitimate purchases along with genuine fraud, a false positive that's its own kind of costly failure. The identical anomaly-scoring principle protects account logins too, watching for unfamiliar devices or login locations that deviate from your normal pattern, not a fixed list of suspicious behaviors. Nobody was watching your card.
