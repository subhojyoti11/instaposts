Three Ways to
Recognize a Face.

🖼️
PHOTO MATCH
Compare pixels
Goal
Check if the new image matches a stored photo.
Focus
Direct pixel-by-pixel or template image comparison.
Result
Fails on lighting, angle, or a printed photo spoof.
📍
LANDMARKS
Compare geometry
Goal
Measure distances between key facial features.
Focus
Tracks fixed points like eyes, nose, and jawline spacing.
Result
Better, but still fooled by a flat photo or mask.
🧠
DEPTH + EMBEDDING
Compare identity, in 3D
Goal
Turn your face into a numerical identity vector.
Focus
An infrared depth map plus a neural network embedding.
Result
Recognizes you across looks, rejects flat photos entirely.
↻ The shift: does this look like you → does this map to the same identity

A Point in
Space, Not a Picture.

Infrared dot projectionThousands of invisible infrared dots are projected onto your face to capture its real 3D shape.
Depth map, not a photoThe sensor builds a 3D depth map, so a flat printed photo or screen has no depth to match.
Face turned into a vectorA neural network converts that 3D shape into a numerical embedding, a point in mathematical space.
Similarity, not equalityUnlocking compares your new embedding's distance to the stored one, allowing small everyday variation.

The Loop, in
Pseudocode.

# One Face ID unlock attempt, simplified def unlock_attempt(depth_map): embedding = face_to_vector(depth_map) distance = cosine_distance(embedding, stored_embedding) return distance < THRESHOLD # close enough, not identical

Your face was never stored as a picture. It's stored as a point in high-dimensional space, and unlocking just checks how close you are to it.

07 / 07

Quick
Reference.

Facial embedding
A numerical vector representing a face's identity, generated by a neural network, not a stored image.
Depth map
A 3D representation of a surface's shape, built from projected infrared light patterns.
Cosine similarity
A way to measure how close two vectors point in the same direction, used to compare embeddings.
Liveness detection
Checks that confirm a real, present face is being scanned, not a photo, mask, or video.

Caption
Your phone has never once compared a photo of your face to another photo. It doesn't store a picture of you at all. Comparing images directly, pixel by pixel, sounds like the obvious way to recognize a face, but it fails constantly in practice. Lighting changes, camera angle shifts, glasses come on and off, and a new haircut all change the raw pixels of your face on every single scan, so exact image matching would reject your own face almost every time you tried to unlock your phone. Tracking the geometric distance between key facial landmarks, like the spacing between your eyes and nose, improves on that, but it's still just analyzing a flat image, which means a printed photo or a video playing on a screen could potentially fool it. What Face ID actually does starts by projecting more than thirty thousand invisible infrared dots onto your face, building a genuine three-dimensional depth map of its actual shape, something a flat photo or screen simply has no depth to fake. A neural network then converts that 3D shape into a facial embedding, a long list of numbers representing a specific point in a high-dimensional mathematical space, one that captures your identity rather than any single image of you. Unlocking your phone means capturing a new scan, generating a fresh embedding, and measuring how close that point is to the one stored during setup, using something like cosine similarity, not checking whether the two are identical. That distance-based comparison is exactly why the system keeps recognizing you as you age, grow a beard, or put on glasses, small variations move your embedding only slightly, still well within the accepted range, while Apple states the odds of a random stranger's face falsely matching at around one in a million. Airport e-gates and secure building access systems run the identical embedding-and-similarity principle, just deployed at a much larger scale. There was never a photo of you locked away in your phone. There's only a point in space, and your face just has to keep landing near it. #faceid #computervision #biometrics #machinelearning #ravenextechnologies
