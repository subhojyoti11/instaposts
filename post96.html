Caption
Tesla did not build a humanoid robot from scratch. It repurposed one it had already been training for years, just wearing a different body. Every robotics company traditionally builds perception, planning, and control as separate systems, hand-tuned per product. Tesla's bet with Optimus was different: the same camera-based vision stack trained for Full Self-Driving could, with a new task head bolted on, drive a humanoid body instead of a car chassis. The expensive, hard part of robotics, learning to understand and predict a 3D world from raw camera video, gets solved once and reused. That shared foundation is the occupancy network, the model responsible for predicting what physical space is occupied and how it will change. In a car, that prediction feeds steering and braking decisions. In Optimus, the same underlying spatial understanding feeds arm and hand movements instead. The perception backbone stays identical. Only the task-specific head on top changes: fsd_head outputs a driving action, optimus_head outputs a manipulation action, both reading from the same learned features. None of this trains on Optimus footage alone, which barely exists compared to the driving fleet. Dojo, Tesla's custom AI training silicon, was built specifically to process real-world video at the scale a global vehicle fleet produces. Millions of miles of driving data become training signal for a system whose vision backbone Optimus inherits directly, even though Optimus itself has never driven a car. The deployment path has been deliberately incremental. Early demonstrations showed Optimus performing tasks like battery cell sorting on an actual Gigafactory production line, not a staged research rig, with the scope of tasks expanding gradually rather than jumping straight to general home use. The bigger claim underneath all of this is architectural, not just commercial: if perception, planning, and physical control can share one backbone across a car and a humanoid, the next robot Tesla builds may not need a new brain at all, just a new body bolted onto the one it already has. #teslaoptimus #humanoidrobotics #selfdrivingcars #dojo #embodiedai #ravenextechnologies
