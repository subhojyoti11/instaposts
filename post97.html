The Internet Has
No Body.

Text and images
Language models trained on trillions of tokens scraped from the open web. That corpus already existed.
Physical actions
No equivalent corpus exists for "how a hand grips an egg without cracking it." That data has to be generated, not scraped.
The bottleneck
Every physical demonstration costs real time, a real robot, and often a real human, which is why embodied AI has lagged years behind language AI.

07 / 07

Quick
Reference.

Teleoperation
A human remotely pilots a physical robot, generating labeled training demonstrations as a byproduct of doing the task.
Domain randomization
Varying simulated lighting, textures, and physics so a policy trained in sim generalizes better to reality.
Sim2real gap
The performance drop when a policy trained in simulation meets the messiness of the real physical world.
Haptic feedback
Force feedback sent back to the pilot's controller, letting them feel resistance and grip strength remotely.

Caption
Language models had it easy. The internet already contained trillions of tokens of text, scraped and ready to train on. Robots have no equivalent corpus. Nobody has ever posted a labeled dataset of exactly how a hand grips an egg without cracking it, and that gap is the real bottleneck in embodied AI. Three sources are racing to fill it. Simulation generates unlimited synthetic demonstrations inside a physics engine, cheap and infinitely scalable, but no simulated physics perfectly matches the real world, a gap in performance known as the sim2real gap. Domain randomization, deliberately varying simulated lighting, textures, and friction during training, narrows that gap but never fully closes it. Real-world deployment logs are the richest signal of all, capturing exactly how a robot handled a genuine edge case, but that data only exists after a robot is already out in the field doing real work, which is precisely the chicken-and-egg problem new robotics companies face. Teleoperation is the bridge. A human pilot puts on a VR headset and haptic-feedback controllers, and their hand and arm movements drive an actual physical robot in real time. Every camera frame, joint position, gripper force, and pilot action gets logged automatically as a labeled demonstration, a byproduct of the pilot simply doing the task. Real physics, real contact forces, real failure modes, captured without needing a robot already deployed in the world. The constraint is throughput: one pilot operates one robot at a time, so companies scale by running dozens of pilots in parallel, effectively building teleoperation data farms. Figure AI and 1X Technologies both run dedicated programs like this specifically to bootstrap the demonstration data their neural policies train on. It has created a genuinely new job category: people paid specifically to generate training data by remotely piloting robots for hours at a time. None of these three sources wins alone. Simulation gives volume, teleoperation gives grounded physical realism, and real-world logs give the edge cases nobody thought to simulate. Combining all three, at scale, is what is actually closing the data gap. #teleoperation #robotics #embodiedai #vr #machinelearning #ravenextechnologies
